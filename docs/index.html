The one idea

Give monceai a table and it learns to predict one column from the others — and, unlike almost every other model, it tells you why, in plain sentences, by pointing at the training rows that look like your query and the exact logical conditions that bind them. One file, one class, nothing but the Python standard library, and a hard O(m·n²) budget.

Everything is built from a single primitive — the literal, a yes/no question about one feature (Age > 37?, Name contains "Mrs"?). Literals OR into clauses, clauses AND into the condition that explains a match. To classify a new point we find its lookalikes: training rows whose learned condition it satisfies. Their class distribution is the prediction; the conditions are the audit trail.

The question this article answers

The original engine picks which feature to split on uniformly at random. That is elegant and provably keeps the O(m·n²) bound — but on a wide table full of junk columns, random opposition spends most of its clauses on noise. monceai adds one thing: a mutual-information-filtered boolean bank that scores every candidate predicate by I(predicate; target) and steers clause construction toward the columns that actually carry signal.

Does MI-steering beat random opposition? We refuse to answer with a single lucky split. Every result below is a paired, same-seed, same-budget comparison across many seeds, reported with a two-sided sign-test — including the cases where steering loses.

But only where it should — the crossover law

The honest finding is a boundary, not a blanket win. We swept the noise width at a fixed 3 signal columns. MI-steering helps once informative columns become a small minority, and is neutral-to-harmful when signal is easy to hit at random:

Two consequences fall straight out of this law, and both were confirmed:

  • NLP is a loss (1W–22L AUROC). A bag-of-words document is a single text column — there is nothing to steer between, so random token opposition already lands on the signal. We report it plainly.
  • Clean tabular data is a tie. On Titanic (a handful of clean columns) MI-steering neither helps nor hurts — within seed noise of the original engine.

What we shipped: mi_rate="auto"

Because the win is conditional, the default must be too. At train time monceai estimates what fraction of columns carry mutual information and engages the bank only when signal is diluted across a wide table. The guarantee:

Beat the original systematically on wide, noisy data — and stay byte-for-byte identical to it on clean, narrow data. 
from monceai import Snake

# auto-detects table shape; engages MI-steering only when it will help
model = Snake("train.csv", target_index=1, n_layers=100)   # mi_rate="auto"
model.predict({"Age": 22, "Sex": "male"})
print(model.audit({"Age": 22, "Sex": "male"}))   # plain-English reasons
model.explain()                                    # global feature importance
model.to_py("frozen.py")                           # standalone, zero-dep inference

Negative results we kept

Good science publishes the nulls. Three inference-side ideas that felt promising and did not survive many-seed sign-tests, all left in the repo as runnable benchmarks:

idearesultverdict
Smarter vote rules (Laplace / specificity)+0.0002 AUROCnull
Compute-fair bagging10W–21L, p=0.07null
Training-tuned decision threshold13W–16L, p=0.71null

They tie because, with steering off, monceai's clause engine is the original up to the random seed — same score ranking, same AUROC. The only lever that moves the needle is changing how clauses are built, which is exactly what the MI bank does.

Open topics & directions

The crossover law raises more questions than it closes. These are open — an invitation, not a roadmap. If one of them pulls you in, the benchmarks are built to be forked.

  • Learn the switch instead of thresholding it. mi_rate="auto" currently flips the bank on below a hand-set fraction of informative columns. Can the blend rate be tuned per-dataset from a held-out fold, or annealed across layers, without leaking?
  • Interaction-aware predicates. The bank scores each column in isolation by I(predicate; target), so it is blind to XOR-style signal that lives in a pair of columns. Pairwise or conditional-MI predicates would catch it — at a cost to the O(m·n²) budget that needs measuring, not assuming.
  • Rescue NLP by manufacturing columns. Text loses because a document is a single column — nothing to steer between. Projecting tokens, n-grams, or embeddings into many derived columns turns text back into the wide-noisy regime where steering wins. Does the crossover law survive the transform?
  • Regression under steering. Every headline number here is classification. The bank bins a continuous target to score MI; how does that interact with the regression path's bin centres, and does steering help on wide noisy regression tables too?
  • Calibration, not just ranking. AUROC measures order; it says nothing about whether predict_proba is calibrated. The lookalike vote is a natural probability — is it honest, and does steering move it?
  • A real benchmark, not synthetic. The wide-noisy win is measured on synthetic data by construction. The obvious next step is an openly-licensed wide, noisy tabular corpus where the crossover law can be confirmed — or falsified — in the wild.
